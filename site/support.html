Support

Lurent turns a photograph into a soft abstract wallpaper. Pick a picture, shape it with the effect controls, set it as your desktop.

Getting started

Choosing a picture

Five sources, switched with the Source menu: search Wikimedia Commons, Pexels or Unsplash, pick from your Photos library, or open a file from your Mac. Unsplash needs an access key of your own, entered under Lurent › Settings…; a Pexels key there is optional. The Liquid style needs no picture at all — it generates its image from the colour controls.

Shaping it

Four styles: blur a photo into a soft gradient, fold it along a mirrored wave, slice it into rhythmic bands, or build a flowing gradient from colour alone. Every control keeps moving — blur, crop, mirror line, slat count and angle, palette, brightness, saturation, contrast, grain — so one photo never has to give you the same wallpaper twice.

Light and dark

A wallpaper has two faces. Use the Light / Dark switch above the preview to design each one, and the button next to it to copy one over to the other as a starting point. macOS swaps between them with your system appearance.

Setting the wallpaper

“Set as Desktop” applies it to every display. Every creation stays in your collection — the strip on the left — so you can go back to an earlier one at any time and put it back.

Every display

One wallpaper covers all of them at maximum resolution, up to a Studio Display XDR — and in full colour, the way your screen shows it. Nothing is upscaled, and nothing is made smaller to fit the weakest screen. The Crop slider decides which part of the photo ends up in the middle.

Projects

Each creation is a .lurent document holding the source photo, the settings and the render. There is no save button: changes are written as you make them. The “+” button duplicates the open project so you can branch off a variation without losing the original.

Where they live

In iCloud, so they follow you between Macs: in iCloud Drive/Lurent/. With iCloud Drive turned off, projects stay local in Application Support.

Renaming

Project names come from the photo, so there is nothing to fill in. If you want your own name, rename the .lurent document in the Finder — Lurent picks that up and stops renaming it for you.

Sharing one

“Export Project…” writes the whole editable document out; whoever opens it gets your settings, not just a finished picture. “Export as HEIC…” gives you the plain wallpaper file instead.

Requirements

macOS 26 or later on Apple silicon.

Get in touch

Bugs, requests, or something that just does not work: open an issue on GitHub or write to hello@lurent.app. Telling us your macOS version and what you were doing helps more than you would think.
